\begin{center}
    \Large \textbf{Extremally projective normed spaces}\\[0.5cm]
    \small {N. T. Nemesh}\\[0.5cm]
\end{center}

\thispagestyle{empty}

\medskip
\textbf{Abstract:} A normed space is called extremally projective if it is
$C$-topologically projective in the category of normed spaces for every $C>1$.
Helemskii proved that the spaces $\lo{\Lambda}$ of finitely supported functions
on a set $\Lambda$ with the $\ell_1$-norm are extremally projective and asked
whether every extremally projective normed space is isometrically isomorphic to
some $\lo{\Lambda}$. We answer this question in the negative. Then we show
that the completion of an extremally projective normed space is isometrically
isomorphic to $\ellone{\Gamma}$ for some set $\Gamma$, and that extremally
projective normed spaces contain no infinite dimensional complete subspaces;
in particular, no infinite dimensional Banach space is extremally projective
in the category of normed spaces. For normed spaces of countable algebraic
dimension these necessary conditions turn out to be sufficient: such a space
$P$ is extremally projective if and only if its completion is isometrically
isomorphic to $\ellone{\Gamma}$ for a countable set $\Gamma$, if and only if
the Banach-Mazur distance between $P$ and $\lo{\Gamma}$ equals $1$. Thus,
extremal projectivity is an almost isometric rather than an isometric
property. In particular, every dense subspace of $\ell_1$ of countable
dimension is extremally projective.
\medskip

\textbf{Keywords:} projective normed space, topological projectivity, extremal
projectivity, Banach-Mazur distance, $\ell_1$-space.

\section{Introduction}\label{SectionIntroduction}

Projectivity in categories of normed and Banach modules admits several
non-equivalent versions. Besides the classical relative projectivity of
Helemskii~\cite{HelHomolBanTopAlg}, which ignores the norms of lifting
operators, there are so-called metric and topological
projectivity~\cite{HelMetrFrQMod, WhiteInjmoduAlg, NemMetrTopHomol}, where the
norm of a lifting is controlled by a constant. In the metric theory this
constant is $1$, and in the topological theory it is some fixed $C\geq 1$. The
present paper is devoted to the limiting case between these two theories: a
module is called extremally projective if it is $C$-topologically projective for
every $C>1$. This notion was introduced and studied by Helemskii under the name
of extreme projectivity~\cite{HelExtrProjSeqAlg, HelMetrFrQMod}. We restrict
ourselves to the simplest possible algebra, namely the zero algebra. Normed
modules over the zero algebra are just normed spaces, so we deal with extremal
projectivity in the category $\Nor$ of normed spaces and bounded linear
operators.

For Banach spaces the corresponding questions are classical. By a theorem of
K\"othe~\cite{KotheTopProjBanSp} topologically projective Banach spaces are
exactly the Banach spaces topologically isomorphic to $\ellone{\Lambda}$ for some
set $\Lambda$, and by a theorem of Grothendieck~\cite{GrothMetrProjFlatBanSp}
a Banach space has the metric lifting property if and only if it is
isometrically isomorphic to some $\ellone{\Lambda}$. For normed spaces the
answers are different, because the category $\Nor$ has its own free objects.
These are the spaces $\lo{\Lambda}$ of finitely supported functions on a set
$\Lambda$ with the $\ell_1$-norm. Gr{\o}nb{\ae}k~\cite{GronLiftProbNormSp}
extended K\"othe's theorem to $\Nor$: a normed space is topologically projective
if and only if it is topologically isomorphic to $\lo{\Lambda}$ for some set
$\Lambda$. Helemskii~\cite{HelMetrFrQMod} proved the metric counterpart: a
normed space is metrically projective if and only if it is isometrically
isomorphic to some $\lo{\Lambda}$. He also showed that the spaces $\lo{\Lambda}$
are extremally projective and asked whether, conversely, every extremally
projective normed space is isometrically isomorphic to some $\lo{\Lambda}$
\cite[section 2]{HelExtrProjSeqAlg}, \cite[section 1]{HelMetrFrQMod}.

The main results of this paper are the following. First, the answer to
Helemskii's question is negative: the subspace of $\ell_1$ spanned by the
finitely supported sequences and a single sequence with infinite support is
extremally projective but not isometrically isomorphic to any $\lo{\Lambda}$.
Second, extremally projective normed spaces have no infinite dimensional
complete subspaces, while their completions are isometrically isomorphic to
the spaces $\ellone{\Gamma}$. In particular, an infinite dimensional Banach
space is never extremally projective in $\Nor$, although it may well be
extremally projective in the category $\Ban$ of Banach spaces. Third, for
normed spaces of countable algebraic dimension extremal projectivity turns out
to be an almost isometric property: such a space $P$ is extremally projective
if and only if its completion is isometrically isomorphic to $\ellone{\Gamma}$
for a countable set $\Gamma$, if and only if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there
is a linear bijection $T:P\to\lo{\Gamma}$ with
$\norm{T}\norm{T^{-1}}<1+\varepsilon$. The second result explains the main
technical difficulty of the subject: when the lifting problems are posed for
all normed spaces rather than Banach spaces, the standard tools of Banach
space geometry, which rely on completeness, are no longer available, and we
must work with the finite dimensional pieces of our spaces directly. The
uncountable case remains open; we give a necessary condition of Baire category
type and examples showing that in this case extremal projectivity is not
determined by the completion.

Let us fix the notation and recall the definitions. All normed spaces are
considered over the complex field; the real case is identical. For a normed
space $E$ we denote by $B_E$ its closed unit ball and by $B_E^\circ$ its open
unit ball. By $\Nor$ we denote the category of normed spaces and bounded linear
operators and by $\Ban$ its full subcategory of Banach spaces. For a set
$\Lambda$ we denote by $\ellone{\Lambda}$ the Banach space of absolutely
summable families and by $\lo{\Lambda}$ its dense subspace consisting of finitely
supported families. For $\lambda\in\Lambda$ the symbol $e_\lambda$ denotes the
family which equals $1$ at $\lambda$ and $0$ elsewhere; the family
${(e_\lambda)}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ is a Hamel basis of $\lo{\Lambda}$. For a
normed space $E$ and a set $\Lambda$ there is a bijection between bounded
families ${(x_\lambda)}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ in $E$ and bounded linear
operators $T:\lo{\Lambda}\to E$, given by $T(e_\lambda)=x_\lambda$, and
$\norm{T}=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\norm{x_\lambda}$. This is the sense in
which the spaces $\lo{\Lambda}$ are free objects of $\Nor$; note that no
completeness of $E$ is required here, in contrast with the case of
$\ellone{\Lambda}$.

Following~\cite{NemMetrTopHomol}, we say that a bounded linear operator
$\xi:X\to Y$ between normed spaces is $c$-topologically surjective, where
$c\geq 1$, if $c\,\xi(B_X^\circ)\supset B_Y^\circ$, and that $\xi$ is
coisometric if it is contractive and $1$-topologically surjective, that is, if
$\xi(B_X^\circ)=B_Y^\circ$. Equivalently, $\xi$ is $c$-topologically surjective
if for every $y\in Y$ and every $\delta>0$ there exists $x\in X$ with
$\xi(x)=y$ and $\norm{x}<c\norm{y}+\delta$. A linear bijection $T:E\to F$ is
called a $c$-isomorphism if $\norm{T}\norm{T^{-1}}\leq c$. The Banach-Mazur
distance between $E$ and $F$ is
\[
\BM(E,F)=\inf\{\norm{T}\norm{T^{-1}}:T:E\to F \mbox{ is a linear bijection}\},
\]
with $\BM(E,F)=+\infty$ if there are no bounded linear bijections with bounded
inverses between $E$ and $F$. A bounded linear operator $\zeta:E\to F$ is
called a $c$-retraction if there exists a bounded linear operator $\eta:F\to E$
with $\zeta\eta=1_F$ and $\norm{\zeta}\norm{\eta}\leq c$. In this case $F$ is
called a $c$-retract of $E$.

\begin{definition}\label{DefTopProj} Let $C\geq 1$. A normed space $P$ is
    called $C$-topologically projective if for every $c\geq 1$, every
    $c$-topologically surjective operator $\xi:X\to Y$ between normed spaces
    and every bounded linear operator $\phi:P\to Y$ there exists a bounded
    linear operator $\psi:P\to X$ such that $\xi\psi=\phi$ and
    $\norm{\psi}\leq cC\norm{\phi}$. A normed space is called topologically
    projective if it is $C$-topologically projective for some $C\geq 1$, and
    extremally projective if it is $C$-topologically projective for every
    $C>1$.
\end{definition}

The operator $\psi$ in definition~\ref{DefTopProj} is called a lifting of
$\phi$ along $\xi$. Extremal projectivity is precisely the extreme projectivity
of Helemskii, see proposition~\ref{EquivWithHelemskii} below. We emphasize that
the spaces $X$ and $Y$ in definition~\ref{DefTopProj} are arbitrary normed
spaces. If we restrict them to Banach spaces, we obtain the notions of
topological and extremal projectivity in $\Ban$, which we shall mention only
for comparison. The two notions differ substantially, see
section~\ref{SectionNecessaryConditions}.

The paper is organized as follows. In section~\ref{SectionBasicTheory} we
collect the basic properties of extremally projective normed spaces: the
spaces $\lo{\Lambda}$ are extremally projective, extremal projectivity passes
to $(1+\varepsilon)$-retracts, and a normed space is extremally projective if
and only if it is a $(1+\varepsilon)$-retract of a free normed space for every
$\varepsilon>0$. In section~\ref{SectionNecessaryConditions} we show that
topologically projective normed spaces do not contain infinite dimensional
Banach subspaces. Section~\ref{SectionCounterexample} contains the
counterexample to Helemskii's question. Section~\ref{SectionMainTheorem} is
devoted to the almost isometric characterization of extremally projective
normed spaces of countable dimension and to the discussion of the uncountable
case.

\section{Basic properties of extremally projective normed
  spaces}\label{SectionBasicTheory}

We start with the free objects of $\Nor$.

\begin{proposition}\label{FreeIsExtrProj} Let $\Lambda$ be a set. Then
    $\lo{\Lambda}$ is $(1+\varepsilon)$-topologically projective for every
    $\varepsilon>0$. In particular, $\lo{\Lambda}$ is extremally projective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Let $\xi:X\to Y$ be a $c$-topologically surjective operator and
    $\phi:\lo{\Lambda}\to Y$ be a bounded linear operator. Fix
    $\varepsilon>0$. For every $\lambda\in\Lambda$ put $y_\lambda=\phi(e_\lambda)$,
    so $\norm{y_\lambda}\leq\norm{\phi}$. If $y_\lambda=0$, put $x_\lambda=0$.
    Otherwise $(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\norm{y_\lambda}^{-1}y_\lambda\in B_Y^\circ$,
    so there exists $x_\lambda'\in B_X^\circ$ with
    $c\,\xi(x_\lambda')=(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\norm{y_\lambda}^{-1}y_\lambda$.
    Put $x_\lambda=c(1+\varepsilon)\norm{y_\lambda}x_\lambda'$, then
    $\xi(x_\lambda)=y_\lambda$ and
    $\norm{x_\lambda}<c(1+\varepsilon)\norm{y_\lambda}\leq
    c(1+\varepsilon)\norm{\phi}$. Since the family
    ${(x_\lambda)}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ is bounded, there is a bounded linear
    operator $\psi:\lo{\Lambda}\to X$ with $\psi(e_\lambda)=x_\lambda$ for all
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$ and $\norm{\psi}=\sup_{\lambda}\norm{x_\lambda}\leq
    c(1+\varepsilon)\norm{\phi}$. Finally, $\xi\psi$ and $\phi$ coincide on
    the Hamel basis ${(e_\lambda)}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$, so $\xi\psi=\phi$.
\end{proof}

The constant $1+\varepsilon$ in proposition~\ref{FreeIsExtrProj} cannot be
replaced by $1$. In fact, with the convention of
definition~\ref{DefTopProj}, $1$-topological projectivity is an empty notion
in $\Nor$.

\begin{proposition}\label{NoOneTopProj} The only $1$-topologically projective
    normed space is the zero space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Let $X$ be the space of finitely supported sequences with the
    $\sup$-norm and $f:X\to\mathbb{C}$ be the functional
    $f(x)=s^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^{-n}(1-2^{-n})x_n$, where
    $s=\sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^{-n}(1-2^{-n})$. Clearly, $\norm{f}\leq 1$. The
    operator $f$ is coisometric: for $z\in B^\circ_\mathbb{C}$ choose $N$ with
    $|z|<s^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^N 2^{-n}(1-2^{-n})$, then $z=f(x)$ for the sequence
    $x=t\,\operatorname{sgn}(z)\sum_{n=1}^N e_n$ with a suitable $t\in[0,1)$,
    and $\norm{x}<1$. On the other hand, $|f(x)|=1$ for $x\in B_X$ would
    force $x_n=\operatorname{sgn}(f(x))$ for all $n$, which is impossible for
    a finitely supported sequence. Thus, $f(B_X)=f(B_X^\circ)=B^\circ_\mathbb{C}$.

    Now let $P$ be a non-zero $1$-topologically projective normed space. Take
    $p\in P$ with $\norm{p}=1$ and, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, a functional
    $g\in P^*$ with $\norm{g}=g(p)=1$. Lifting $g$ along the coisometric
    operator $f$ we get an operator $\psi:P\to X$ with $f\psi=g$ and
    $\norm{\psi}\leq 1$. Then $x=\psi(p)\in B_X$ and $f(x)=g(p)=1$, which
    contradicts the previous paragraph.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{NoOneTopProj} shows why in $\Nor$ it is natural to consider
all constants $C>1$ at once, instead of the single constant $C=1$ of the
metric theory.

\begin{remark}\label{RemarkStrictConvention} The proof of
    proposition~\ref{NoOneTopProj} uses only the fact that the coisometric
    functional $f$ does not attain its norm. Functionals of this kind exist on
    Banach spaces too, for example $x\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^\infty(1-2^{-n})x_n$ on
    $\ell_1$. Therefore, if $c$-topologically surjective operators are defined
    via open balls as in definition~\ref{DefTopProj}, then $1$-topological
    projectivity is an empty notion in $\Ban$ as well, and the free Banach
    spaces $\ellone{\Lambda}$ are only $(1+\varepsilon)$-topologically projective
    for every $\varepsilon>0$. The metric theory of \cite{HelMetrFrQMod} avoids
    this problem by posing lifting problems along strictly coisometric
    operators, that is, operators mapping the closed unit ball onto the closed
    unit ball; with this convention $\ellone{\Lambda}$ and $\lo{\Lambda}$ are
    $1$-topologically projective in $\Ban$ and $\Nor$ respectively, and
    $1$-topological projectivity coincides with metric projectivity, as in
    \cite[proposition 2.1.9]{NemMetrTopHomol}. For the purposes of this paper
    the choice of convention is immaterial: if we call $\xi:X\to Y$ strictly
    $c$-topologically surjective whenever $c\,\xi(B_X)\supset B_Y$ and define
    strict $C$-topological projectivity by lifting along such operators with
    the same bound $\norm{\psi}\leq cC\norm{\phi}$, then every
    $C$-topologically projective space is strictly $C$-topologically projective,
    and every strictly $C$-topologically projective space is
    $C(1+\varepsilon)$-topologically projective for every $\varepsilon>0$. The
    first claim is obvious, since strictly $c$-topologically surjective
    operators are $c$-topologically surjective. For the second one, given a
    $c$-topologically surjective operator $\xi:X\to Y$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
    endow $X$ with the equivalent norm
    $\norm{x}''=\max(\norm{x},(1+\varepsilon)c\norm{\xi(x)})$; then
    $(1+\varepsilon)c\xi$ becomes a contractive operator mapping the closed
    unit ball onto the closed unit ball, and a lifting $\psi''$ along it with
    $\norm{\psi''}''\leq C\norm{\phi}$ gives the lifting
    $\psi=(1+\varepsilon)c\psi''$ along $\xi$ with
    $\norm{\psi}\leq(1+\varepsilon)cC\norm{\phi}$. In particular, the two
    conventions lead to the same notion of extremal projectivity.
\end{remark}

Our definition of extremal projectivity is equivalent to Helemskii's definition
of extreme projectivity~\cite[definition 1.3]{HelExtrProjSeqAlg}: a normed
space $P$ is extremely projective if for every coisometric operator
$\xi:X\to Y$, every bounded linear operator $\phi:P\to Y$ and every
$\delta>0$ there exists a lifting $\psi:P\to X$ of $\phi$ along $\xi$ with
$\norm{\psi}<\norm{\phi}+\delta$.

\begin{proposition}\label{EquivWithHelemskii} A normed space is extremally
    projective if and only if it is extremely projective in the sense of
    Helemskii.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Let $P$ be extremally projective, $\xi:X\to Y$ be a coisometric
    operator, $\phi:P\to Y$ be a bounded linear operator and $\delta>0$. If
    $\phi=0$ we take $\psi=0$. Otherwise we put
    $C=1+\delta\norm{\phi}^{-1}/2$. Since $\xi$ is $1$-topologically surjective,
    $C$-topological projectivity of $P$ gives a lifting $\psi$ of $\phi$ along
    $\xi$ with $\norm{\psi}\leq C\norm{\phi}<\norm{\phi}+\delta$.

    Conversely, let $P$ be extremely projective, $C>1$, $\xi:X\to Y$ be a
    $c$-topologically surjective operator and $\phi:P\to Y$ be a non-zero
    bounded linear operator. Consider the operator $\xi'=c\xi$ and endow the
    vector space $X$ with the norm $\norm{x}'=\max(\norm{x},\norm{\xi'(x)})$,
    which is equivalent to the original one since $\norm{x}\leq\norm{x}'\leq
    \max(1,c\norm{\xi})\norm{x}$. Denote the resulting normed space by $X'$.
    The operator $\xi':X'\to Y$ is contractive by construction. If $y\in
    B_Y^\circ$, then by $c$-topological surjectivity of $\xi$ there is $x\in
    B_X^\circ$ with $\xi'(x)=y$, and $\norm{x}'=\max(\norm{x},\norm{y})<1$.
    Thus, $\xi':X'\to Y$ is coisometric. By extreme projectivity there is a
    lifting $\psi':P\to X'$ of $\phi$ along $\xi'$ with $\norm{\psi'}<
    \norm{\phi}+(C-1)\norm{\phi}=C\norm{\phi}$. Regarded as an operator into
    $X$, the operator $\psi=c\psi'$ satisfies $\xi\psi=\xi'\psi'=\phi$ and
    $\norm{\psi}\leq c\norm{\psi'}'\leq cC\norm{\phi}$. Since $C>1$ is arbitrary,
    $P$ is extremally projective.
\end{proof}

Next we record the standard permanence properties. The proofs are the same as
in the Banach case, see \cite[lemma 2.7]{WhiteInjmoduAlg} or
\cite[proposition 2.1.3]{NemMetrTopHomol}, and we include them only for
completeness.

\begin{proposition}\label{RetractOfProj} Let $P'$ be a $C$-topologically
    projective normed space and $P$ be a $c$-retract of $P'$. Then $P$ is
    $cC$-topologically projective. Consequently, a normed space which is a
    $(1+\varepsilon)$-retract of an extremally projective normed space for every
    $\varepsilon>0$ is extremally projective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Let $\zeta:P'\to P$ and $\eta:P\to P'$ be bounded linear
    operators with $\zeta\eta=1_P$ and $\norm{\zeta}\norm{\eta}\leq c$.
    Consider a $c'$-topologically surjective operator $\xi:X\to Y$ and a
    bounded linear operator $\phi:P\to Y$. By $C$-topological projectivity of
    $P'$ there is a lifting $\psi':P'\to X$ of $\phi\zeta$ along $\xi$ with
    $\norm{\psi'}\leq c'C\norm{\phi\zeta}$. Then $\psi=\psi'\eta$ satisfies
    $\xi\psi=\phi\zeta\eta=\phi$ and $\norm{\psi}\leq
    c'C\norm{\phi}\norm{\zeta}\norm{\eta}\leq c'cC\norm{\phi}$.
\end{proof}

For a normed space $P$ consider the operator
\[
\pi_P:\lo{B_P}\to P,\quad e_x\mapsto x.
\]
It is well defined and contractive, because the family
${(x)}_{x\in B_P}$ is bounded by $1$. Moreover, $\pi_P$ maps the closed unit
ball of $\lo{B_P}$ onto $B_P$, since $\pi_P(e_x)=x$ for $x\in B_P$. Thus,
$\pi_P$ is strictly coisometric and, a fortiori, coisometric.

\begin{proposition}\label{CanonicalMorphismCriterion} Let $P$ be a normed
    space and $C\geq 1$. Then
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]
        \item if $P$ is $C$-topologically projective, then $\pi_P$ has a right
              inverse bounded linear operator of norm at most $C$;
        \item if $\pi_P$ has a right inverse bounded linear operator of norm at
              most $C$, then $P$ is $C(1+\varepsilon)$-topologically projective
              for every $\varepsilon>0$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} $(i)$ Lift $1_P$ along the $1$-topologically surjective operator
    $\pi_P$: we get $\sigma:P\to\lo{B_P}$ with $\pi_P\sigma=1_P$ and
    $\norm{\sigma}\leq C$.

    $(ii)$ If $\pi_P\sigma=1_P$ with $\norm{\sigma}\leq C$, then $P$ is a
    $C$-retract of $\lo{B_P}$, which is $(1+\varepsilon)$-topologically
    projective by proposition~\ref{FreeIsExtrProj}. It remains to apply
    proposition~\ref{RetractOfProj}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{ExtrProjCriterion} For a normed space $P$ the following
    conditions are equivalent:
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]
        \item $P$ is extremally projective;
        \item for every $\varepsilon>0$ the operator $\pi_P$ has a right
              inverse bounded linear operator of norm at most $1+\varepsilon$;
        \item for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a set $\Lambda$ such that $P$
              is a $(1+\varepsilon)$-retract of $\lo{\Lambda}$;
        \item for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are a set $\Lambda$, a subspace
              $Z\subset\lo{\Lambda}$ and a projection $Q:\lo{\Lambda}\to\lo{\Lambda}$
              onto $Z$ with $\norm{Q}\leq 1+\varepsilon$ such that
              $\BM(P,Z)\leq 1+\varepsilon$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} $(i)\implies(ii)$ is proposition~\ref{CanonicalMorphismCriterion}~$(i)$,
    and $(ii)\implies(iii)$ is obvious.

    $(iii)\implies(iv)$ Let $\zeta:\lo{\Lambda}\to P$ and $\eta:P\to\lo{\Lambda}$
    satisfy $\zeta\eta=1_P$ and $\norm{\zeta}\norm{\eta}\leq 1+\varepsilon$. Replacing
    $\zeta$ by $\norm{\zeta}^{-1}\zeta$ and $\eta$ by $\norm{\zeta}\eta$ we may
    assume that $\norm{\zeta}=1$ and $\norm{\eta}\leq 1+\varepsilon$. Put
    $Z=\eta(P)$ and $Q=\eta\zeta$. Then $Q^2=\eta(\zeta\eta)\zeta=Q$,
    $\Im(Q)=Z$ and $\norm{Q}\leq 1+\varepsilon$. The operator $\eta:P\to Z$
    is a bijection with $\norm{\eta}\leq 1+\varepsilon$ and
    $\eta^{-1}=\zeta|_Z$, so $\norm{\eta^{-1}}\leq 1$ and
    $\BM(P,Z)\leq 1+\varepsilon$.

    $(iv)\implies(i)$ Let $T:P\to Z$ be a linear bijection with
    $\norm{T}\norm{T^{-1}}\leq 1+\varepsilon$. Put $\eta=T$ and
    $\zeta=T^{-1}Q:\lo{\Lambda}\to P$. Then $\zeta\eta=T^{-1}QT=T^{-1}T=1_P$ and
    $\norm{\zeta}\norm{\eta}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^2$. By
    propositions~\ref{FreeIsExtrProj} and~\ref{RetractOfProj} the space $P$ is
    $(1+\varepsilon)^3$-topologically projective. As $\varepsilon>0$ is
    arbitrary, $P$ is extremally projective.
\end{proof}

We finish this section with a simple way of constructing new extremally
projective spaces from old ones. For a family ${(P_\lambda)}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$
of normed spaces we denote by $\bigoplus_1^0\{P_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\}$
the subspace of the $\ell_1$-sum $\bigoplus_1\{P_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\}$
consisting of families with finitely many non-zero members. Clearly,
$\bigoplus_1^0\{\mathbb{C}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}=\lo{\Lambda}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{SumsOfExtrProj} Let ${(P_\lambda)}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$
    be a family of extremally projective normed spaces. Then
    $\bigoplus_1^0\{P_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\}$ is extremally projective.
    If $\Lambda$ is finite, then
    $\bigoplus_1\{P_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\}$ is extremally projective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Fix $\varepsilon>0$. By theorem~\ref{ExtrProjCriterion} for
    every $\lambda\in\Lambda$ there are a set $S_\lambda$ and operators
    $\zeta_\lambda:\lo{S_\lambda}\to P_\lambda$, $\eta_\lambda:P_\lambda\to\lo{S_\lambda}$
    with $\zeta_\lambda\eta_\lambda=1_{P_\lambda}$, $\norm{\zeta_\lambda}\leq 1$
    and $\norm{\eta_\lambda}\leq 1+\varepsilon$. Let $S$ be the disjoint union of
    the sets $S_\lambda$. Then
    $\lo{S}=\bigoplus_1^0\{\lo{S_\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}$ isometrically,
    and the operators $\zeta=\bigoplus_1^0\zeta_\lambda$ and
    $\eta=\bigoplus_1^0\eta_\lambda$ are well defined bounded linear operators
    between $\lo{S}$ and $P=\bigoplus_1^0\{P_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\}$ with
    $\zeta\eta=1_P$, $\norm{\zeta}\leq 1$ and $\norm{\eta}\leq 1+\varepsilon$.
    By theorem~\ref{ExtrProjCriterion} the space $P$ is extremally projective.
    For finite $\Lambda$ the sums $\bigoplus_1$ and $\bigoplus_1^0$ coincide.
\end{proof}

\section{Topologically projective normed spaces have no large complete
  subspaces}\label{SectionNecessaryConditions}

The free normed spaces $\lo{\Lambda}$ are far from being complete. In this
section we show that this incompleteness is inherited by all topologically
projective normed spaces. The main tool is the Baire category theorem applied
to the finite dimensional pieces of $\lo{\Lambda}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{CompleteSubspOfFree} Let $S$ be a set and $Z$ be a
    subspace of $\lo{S}$ which is complete with respect to some norm
    $\norm{\cdot}'$ satisfying $\norm{z}\leq K\norm{z}'$ for all $z\in Z$ and
    some constant $K>0$. Then $Z$ is finite dimensional.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Assume that $Z$ is infinite dimensional. Choose a linearly
    independent sequence ${(z_n)}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ in $Z$ and let $Z_0$ be
    the closure of its linear span in $(Z,\norm{\cdot}')$. Then
    $(Z_0,\norm{\cdot}')$ is a separable infinite dimensional Banach space.
    The set $S_0=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\supp(z_n)$ is countable, and every
    element of $\Lin\{z_n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is supported in $S_0$. Since the
    coordinate functionals $x\mapsto x(s)$, $s\in S$, are continuous with
    respect to $\norm{\cdot}$ and a fortiori with respect to $\norm{\cdot}'$,
    every element of $Z_0$ is supported in $S_0$ too. As $Z_0$ is infinite
    dimensional, the set $S_0$ is infinite, so we may enumerate it as
    $S_0=\{s_1,s_2,\ldots\}$. For $n\in\mathbb{N}$ put
    \[
    E_n=\{z\in Z_0:\supp(z)\subset\{s_1,\ldots,s_n\}\}
    =Z_0\cap\Lin\{e_{s_1},\ldots,e_{s_n}\}.
    \]
    Each $E_n$ is a finite dimensional, hence closed, subspace of
    $(Z_0,\norm{\cdot}')$, and $Z_0=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_n$ because the
    elements of $Z_0\subset\lo{S}$ are finitely supported. By the Baire
    category theorem some $E_n$ has non-empty interior in $Z_0$, so
    $E_n=Z_0$ and $Z_0$ is finite dimensional. This contradiction proves the
    lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{NoCompleteSubspaces} Let $P$ be a topologically
    projective normed space, $Z$ be a subspace of $P$ and $\norm{\cdot}'$ be a
    norm on $Z$ such that $(Z,\norm{\cdot}')$ is complete and
    $\norm{z}\leq K\norm{z}'$ for all $z\in Z$ and some constant $K>0$. Then
    $Z$ is finite dimensional. In particular, every complete subspace of $P$
    is finite dimensional.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} By proposition~\ref{CanonicalMorphismCriterion}~$(i)$ there
    exists a bounded linear operator $\sigma:P\to\lo{B_P}$ with
    $\pi_P\sigma=1_P$. Since $\pi_P$ is contractive, $\norm{x}\leq\norm{\sigma(x)}$
    for all $x\in P$, so $\sigma$ is injective and
    $\norm{\sigma^{-1}(y)}\leq\norm{y}$ for $y\in\sigma(P)$. Consider the
    subspace $\sigma(Z)$ of $\lo{B_P}$ with the norm
    $\norm{y}''=\norm{\sigma^{-1}(y)}'$. Then $(\sigma(Z),\norm{\cdot}'')$ is
    complete, being isometric to $(Z,\norm{\cdot}')$, and
    $\norm{y}\leq\norm{\sigma}\norm{\sigma^{-1}(y)}\leq K\norm{\sigma}\norm{y}''$
    for $y\in\sigma(Z)$. By lemma~\ref{CompleteSubspOfFree} the space
    $\sigma(Z)$ is finite dimensional, and so is $Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{BanachNotProjInNor} No infinite dimensional Banach
    space is topologically projective in $\Nor$. In particular, for an
    infinite set $\Lambda$ the space $\ellone{\Lambda}$, which is metrically
    projective in $\Ban$, is not even topologically projective in $\Nor$.
\end{corollary}

For modules over sequence algebras this phenomenon was observed by Helemskii
\cite{HelExtrProjSeqAlg}. Theorem~\ref{NoCompleteSubspaces} says more: a
topologically projective normed space cannot even contain an infinite
dimensional subspace that is complete with respect to a stronger norm. The
following example shows that this stronger form is useful. It also shows
that topological projectivity of a normed space is not determined by its
completion.

\begin{example}\label{WeightedExample} Let ${(w_n)}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a
    sequence of real numbers with $w_n\geq 1$ and $\lim_n w_n=+\infty$, and let
    \[
    P_w=\Bigl\{x\in\ell_1:\sum_{n=1}^\infty w_n|x_n|<+\infty\Bigr\}
    \]
    with the norm inherited from $\ell_1$. Then $P_w$ is a dense subspace of
    $\ell_1$ containing $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$, so its completion is $\ell_1$, and
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]
        \item every complete subspace of $P_w$ is finite dimensional;
        \item $P_w$ is not topologically projective.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{example}
\begin{proof} Denote $\norm{x}_w=\sum_{n=1}^\infty w_n|x_n|$ for $x\in P_w$.
    Then $(P_w,\norm{\cdot}_w)$ is the Banach space $\ell_1(w)$ of
    $w$-weighted summable sequences and $\norm{x}\leq\norm{x}_w$ for $x\in
    P_w$.

    $(i)$ Let $Z$ be a complete subspace of $P_w$. If ${(z_k)}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}$
    is a sequence in $Z$ converging to $z\in P_w$ with respect to
    $\norm{\cdot}_w$, then it converges to $z$ in $\ell_1$, so $z\in Z$. Thus,
    $Z$ is closed in $(P_w,\norm{\cdot}_w)$ and the two norms $\norm{\cdot}$
    and $\norm{\cdot}_w$ are complete on $Z$. Since $\norm{\cdot}\leq
    \norm{\cdot}_w$, by the open mapping theorem there is a constant $K$ with
    $\norm{z}_w\leq K\norm{z}$ for all $z\in Z$. Assume that $Z$ is infinite
    dimensional and fix $N\in\mathbb{N}$. The linear map $Z\to\mathbb{C}^N$,
    $z\mapsto(z_1,\ldots,z_N)$ has a non-trivial kernel, so there exists $z\in
    Z$ with $\norm{z}=1$ and $z_n=0$ for $n\leq N$. Then
    $K\geq\norm{z}_w=\sum_{n>N}w_n|z_n|\geq\inf_{n>N}w_n$. Since $N$ is
    arbitrary and $\lim_n w_n=+\infty$, we get a contradiction.

    $(ii)$ Apply theorem~\ref{NoCompleteSubspaces} to $Z=P_w$ and
    $\norm{\cdot}'=\norm{\cdot}_w$.
\end{proof}

\section{Extremally projective spaces which are not free}\label{SectionCounterexample}

In this section we show that extremal projectivity is not an isometric
invariant of the form ``$P$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\lo{\Lambda}$''.
Our examples are subspaces of $\ell_1=\ellone{\mathbb{N}}$ containing
$\lo{\mathbb{N}}$. For a sequence $x\in\ell_1$ and $N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$
we denote by $x|_N$ the sequence which coincides with $x$ at $n\leq N$ and
vanishes at $n>N$. For a finite dimensional subspace $V$ of $\ell_1$ we
consider the normed space
\[
P_V=\lo{\mathbb{N}}+V\subset\ell_1
\]
with the norm inherited from $\ell_1$. Clearly, $\lo{\mathbb{N}}\subset
P_V\subset\ell_1$, so $P_V$ is dense in $\ell_1$. Since $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ has
countable algebraic dimension, so does $P_V$.

\begin{theorem}\label{OneVectorTheorem} Let $v\in\ell_1$,
    $N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$ and $0<\delta<1$. Then there exists a bounded
    linear operator $R:\ell_1\to\ell_1$ with $\norm{R}\leq\delta$ such that
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]
        \item $R(x)=0$ for every $x\in\ell_1$ with $x=x|_N$;
        \item $R(\lo{\mathbb{N}})\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}$;
        \item $(1_{\ell_1}-R)(v)\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$;
        \item ${(1_{\ell_1}-R)}^{-1}(\lo{\mathbb{N}})\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}+\mathbb{C}v$.
    \end{enumerate}
    In particular, $T=1_{\ell_1}-R$ is a linear bijection of $\ell_1$ with
    $\norm{T}\leq 1+\delta$ and $\norm{T^{-1}}\leq{(1-\delta)}^{-1}$ such
    that $T(x)=x$ whenever $x=x|_N$, $T(\lo{\mathbb{N}})\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}$
    and $T(\lo{\mathbb{N}}+\mathbb{C}v)=\lo{\mathbb{N}}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Since $\norm{R}\leq\delta<1$, the operator $T=1_{\ell_1}-R$ is a
    linear bijection with $T^{-1}=\sum_{m=0}^\infty R^m$,
    $\norm{T}\leq 1+\delta$ and $\norm{T^{-1}}\leq{(1-\delta)}^{-1}$, and the
    properties of $T$ listed in the last sentence follow from $(i)$--$(iv)$.
    So we only need to construct $R$ with the properties $(i)$--$(iv)$.

    If $v-v|_N$ is finitely supported, take $R=0$. Assume that $v-v|_N$ has
    infinite support. For $M\geq N$ put $t_M=\norm{v-v|_M}=\sum_{n>M}|v_n|$.
    Then the sequence ${(t_M)}_{M\geq N}$ is non-increasing, $t_M>0$ for all
    $M\geq N$ because $v-v|_N$ has infinite support, and $\lim_M t_M=0$.
    Choose integers $N=N_0<N_1<N_2<\ldots$ such that
    $t_{N_k}\leq(\delta/2)t_{N_{k-1}}$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$. The sets
    $B_k=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:N_{k-1}<n\leq N_k\}$, $k\in\mathbb{N}$, are finite,
    pairwise disjoint and cover $\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n>N\}$. Put
    $w_k=v|_{N_k}-v|_{N_{k-1}}$, that is, $w_k$ is the restriction of $v$ to
    $B_k$. Then $w_k\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$, $v-v|_N=\sum_{k=1}^\infty w_k$ in
    $\ell_1$, $\supp(w_k)\subset B_k$, and
    \[
    \norm{w_k}=t_{N_{k-1}}-t_{N_k}\geq(1-\delta/2)t_{N_{k-1}}>0,\qquad
    \norm{w_{k+1}}\leq t_{N_k}\leq(\delta/2)t_{N_{k-1}}\leq
    \frac{\delta/2}{1-\delta/2}\norm{w_k}\leq\delta\norm{w_k},
    \]
    where the last inequality holds because $\delta\leq 1$. For
    $k\in\mathbb{N}$ define the functional
    \[
    g_k:\ell_1\to\mathbb{C},\quad
    g_k(x)=\sum_{n\in B_k}\overline{\operatorname{sgn}(v_n)}\,x_n,
    \]
    where $\operatorname{sgn}(z)=z/|z|$ for $z\neq 0$ and
    $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$. Then $|g_k(x)|\leq\sum_{n\in B_k}|x_n|$ for
    all $x\in\ell_1$, $g_k(w_k)=\norm{w_k}$ and $g_k(w_j)=0$ for $j\neq k$,
    because the supports of $w_j$ and $g_k$ are disjoint. Now define
    \[
    R:\ell_1\to\ell_1,\quad
    R(x)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{g_k(x)}{\norm{w_k}}\,w_{k+1}.
    \]
    The series converges absolutely, since
    \[
    \sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{|g_k(x)|}{\norm{w_k}}\norm{w_{k+1}}
    \leq\delta\sum_{k=1}^\infty\sum_{n\in B_k}|x_n|=\delta\norm{x},
    \]
    so $R$ is a well defined bounded linear operator with $\norm{R}\leq\delta$.
    We verify the properties $(i)$--$(iv)$.

    $(i)$ If $x=x|_N$, then $g_k(x)=0$ for all $k$, because
    $\supp(g_k)\subset B_k\subset\{n:n>N\}$. Hence $R(x)=0$.

    $(ii)$ If $x\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$, then $g_k(x)\neq 0$ only for the finitely
    many $k$ with $B_k\cap\supp(x)\neq\varnothing$, so $R(x)$ is a finite
    linear combination of the finitely supported sequences $w_{k+1}$.

    $(iii)$ Since $g_k(v|_N)=0$ and
    $g_k(v-v|_N)=\sum_{j=1}^\infty g_k(w_j)=g_k(w_k)=\norm{w_k}$, we get
    $R(v)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty w_{k+1}=v-v|_N-w_1$, so
    $(1_{\ell_1}-R)(v)=v|_N+w_1\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$.

    $(iv)$ Let $x\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ and let $K$ be the finite set of those
    $k$ for which $g_k(x)\neq 0$. Put $c_k=g_k(x)/\norm{w_k}$ for $k\in K$.
    Since $g_j(w_k)=\delta_{jk}\norm{w_k}$, we have $R(w_k)=w_{k+1}$ for all
    $k$, hence $R^m(x)=\sum_{k\in K}c_k w_{k+m}$ for $m\geq 1$ and
    \[
    {(1_{\ell_1}-R)}^{-1}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^\infty R^m(x)
    =x+\sum_{k\in K}c_k\sum_{m=1}^\infty w_{k+m}
    =x+\sum_{k\in K}c_k(v-v|_{N_k})\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}+\mathbb{C}v.
    \]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{FiniteDimPerturbation} Let $V$ be a finite dimensional
    subspace of $\ell_1$. Then $\BM(P_V,\lo{\mathbb{N}})=1$. Consequently,
    $P_V$ is extremally projective.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} We use induction on $m=\dim V$. If $m=0$, then
    $P_V=\lo{\mathbb{N}}$. Let $m\geq 1$, fix $0<\delta<1$ and write
    $V=\mathbb{C}v+V'$ with $\dim V'=m-1$. Let $T$ be the operator given by
    theorem~\ref{OneVectorTheorem} for $v$, $N=0$ and $\delta$. Then
    \[
    T(P_V)=T(\lo{\mathbb{N}}+\mathbb{C}v)+T(V')=\lo{\mathbb{N}}+T(V')=P_{T(V')},
    \]
    and $T(V')$ is a subspace of $\ell_1$ of dimension $m-1$. The restriction
    of $T$ to $P_V$ is a linear bijection onto $P_{T(V')}$ with
    $\norm{T|_{P_V}}\norm{(T|_{P_V})^{-1}}\leq(1+\delta)/(1-\delta)$, so
    $\BM(P_V,\lo{\mathbb{N}})\leq\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\BM(P_{T(V')},\lo{\mathbb{N}})
    =\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}$ by the induction hypothesis. Since $\delta$ is
    arbitrary, $\BM(P_V,\lo{\mathbb{N}})=1$.

    Now let $\varepsilon>0$ and let $S:P_V\to\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ be a linear
    bijection with $\norm{S}\norm{S^{-1}}\leq 1+\varepsilon$. Then $P_V$ is a
    $(1+\varepsilon)$-retract of $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ via $S^{-1}$ and $S$, and by
    propositions~\ref{FreeIsExtrProj} and~\ref{RetractOfProj} it is
    ${(1+\varepsilon)}^2$-topologically projective. As $\varepsilon$ is
    arbitrary, $P_V$ is extremally projective.
\end{proof}

To see that the spaces $P_V$ with $V\not\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ are not
isometrically isomorphic to free normed spaces we look at extreme points of
unit balls. By $\ext(B)$ we denote the set of extreme points of a convex set
$B$ and by $\conv(M)$ the convex hull of a set $M$.

\begin{lemma}\label{ExtremePointsLemma} Let $S$ be a set and $E$ be a
    subspace of $\ellone{S}$ containing $\lo{S}$, endowed with the norm of
    $\ellone{S}$. Then
    $\ext(B_E)=\{\lambda e_s:s\in S,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ |\lambda|=1\}$
    and $\conv(\ext(B_E))=B_{\lo{S}}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} The points $\lambda e_s$ with $|\lambda|=1$ are extreme points
    of $B_{\ellone{S}}$, and $B_E=B_{\ellone{S}}\cap E$, so they are extreme
    points of $B_E$. Conversely, let $x\in B_E$ be not of this form. If
    $x=0$, then $x=\frac12 e_s+\frac12(-e_s)$ for any $s\in S$. If
    $0<\norm{x}<1$, then $x=\frac12(1+t)x+\frac12(1-t)x$ for $t=1-\norm{x}>0$,
    and both $(1\pm t)x$ belong to $B_E$. If $\norm{x}=1$, then $x$ has at
    least two non-zero coordinates $x_n$ and $x_m$, $n\neq m$. Put
    $t=\min(|x_n|,|x_m|)>0$ and
    $u=t(\operatorname{sgn}(x_n)e_n-\operatorname{sgn}(x_m)e_m)\in\lo{S}\subset E$.
    Then $x\pm u\in E$ and
    \[
    \norm{x\pm u}=(|x_n|\pm t)+(|x_m|\mp t)+\sum_{s\neq n,m}|x_s|=\norm{x}=1,
    \]
    so $x=\frac12(x+u)+\frac12(x-u)$ with $u\neq 0$. In all three cases $x$ is
    not an extreme point of $B_E$.

    Every finite convex combination of the points $\lambda e_s$ is a finitely
    supported element of $B_{\ellone{S}}$, so $\conv(\ext(B_E))\subset
    B_{\lo{S}}$. Conversely, if $x\in B_{\lo{S}}$, then
    \[
    x=\sum_{s\in\supp(x)}|x_s|\,\operatorname{sgn}(x_s)e_s+(1-\norm{x})\cdot 0,
    \]
    and $0=\frac12 e_s+\frac12(-e_s)$, so $x\in\conv(\ext(B_E))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{NotIsometricToFree} Let $S$ be a set and $E$ be a
    subspace of $\ellone{S}$ containing $\lo{S}$, endowed with the norm of
    $\ellone{S}$. If $E$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\lo{\Lambda}$ for
    some set $\Lambda$, then $E=\lo{S}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Let $T:\lo{\Lambda}\to E$ be an isometric isomorphism. Then
    $T(B_{\lo{\Lambda}})=B_E$ and $T$ maps $\ext(B_{\lo{\Lambda}})$ onto
    $\ext(B_E)$. Applying lemma~\ref{ExtremePointsLemma} to $E$ and to
    $\lo{\Lambda}$ itself we get
    \[
    B_E=T(B_{\lo{\Lambda}})=T(\conv(\ext(B_{\lo{\Lambda}})))
    =\conv(T(\ext(B_{\lo{\Lambda}})))=\conv(\ext(B_E))=B_{\lo{S}}.
    \]
    Hence $E=\lo{S}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{CounterexampleCorollary} Let $V$ be a finite
    dimensional subspace of $\ell_1$ with $V\not\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}$, for
    example $V=\mathbb{C}v$ for $v={(2^{-n})}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$. Then $P_V$ is
    an extremally projective normed space which is not isometrically
    isomorphic to $\lo{\Lambda}$ for any set $\Lambda$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof} Combine theorems~\ref{FiniteDimPerturbation}
    and~\ref{NotIsometricToFree}.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{CounterexampleCorollary} answers the question of Helemskii
from~\cite[section 2]{HelExtrProjSeqAlg} and~\cite[section 1]{HelMetrFrQMod}
in the negative. Note that by theorem~\ref{NotIsometricToFree} every subspace
$E$ with $\lo{\mathbb{N}}\subsetneq E\subset\ell_1$ fails to be isometrically
isomorphic to a free normed space, but by example~\ref{WeightedExample} not
all of these subspaces are extremally projective. Thus, between
$\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ and $\ell_1$ there are both extremally projective and
non-projective normed spaces, and the difference between them is invisible on
the level of completions. The next section provides a criterion which
distinguishes them.

\section{Extremally projective normed spaces of countable
  dimension}\label{SectionMainTheorem}

We start with a necessary condition which holds in all dimensions. Recall that
the completion $\overline{P}$ of a normed space $P$ is a Banach space containing
$P$ as a dense subspace. Every bounded linear operator between normed spaces
extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator between their completions with
the same norm, and these extensions respect compositions.

\begin{theorem}\label{CompletionIsEllOne} Let $P$ be an extremally projective
    normed space. Then $\overline{P}$ is isometrically isomorphic to
    $\ellone{\Gamma}$ for some set $\Gamma$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Fix $\varepsilon>0$. By theorem~\ref{ExtrProjCriterion} there is
    a bounded linear operator $\sigma:P\to\lo{B_P}$ with $\pi_P\sigma=1_P$ and
    $\norm{\sigma}\leq 1+\varepsilon$. The completion of $\lo{B_P}$ is
    $\ellone{B_P}$, so $\pi_P$ and $\sigma$ extend to bounded linear operators
    $\overline{\pi}:\ellone{B_P}\to\overline{P}$ and
    $\overline{\sigma}:\overline{P}\to\ellone{B_P}$ with
    $\overline{\pi}\,\overline{\sigma}=1_{\overline{P}}$,
    $\norm{\overline{\pi}}\leq 1$ and $\norm{\overline{\sigma}}\leq 1+\varepsilon$.
    Thus, $\overline{P}$ is a $(1+\varepsilon)$-retract of $\ellone{B_P}$ in
    $\Ban$. The proof of proposition~\ref{FreeIsExtrProj} shows that
    $\ellone{\Lambda}$ is $(1+\varepsilon)$-topologically projective in $\Ban$
    for every set $\Lambda$: in the notation of that proof, for a Banach space
    $X$ the family ${(x_\lambda)}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ defines a bounded
    linear operator $\psi:\ellone{\Lambda}\to X$,
    $\psi(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}x(\lambda)x_\lambda$, where the series
    converges absolutely. The proof of proposition~\ref{RetractOfProj} works
    in $\Ban$ verbatim, so $\overline{P}$ is
    ${(1+\varepsilon)}^2$-topologically projective in $\Ban$. Since
    $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, $\overline{P}$ is extremally projective in
    $\Ban$. The proof of proposition~\ref{EquivWithHelemskii} also works in
    $\Ban$ verbatim, because the space $X'$ constructed there is complete
    whenever $X$ is. Hence $\overline{P}$ is extremely projective in $\Ban$ in
    the sense of Helemskii, and by Grothendieck's theorem in the form given
    in~\cite[section 2]{HelExtrProjSeqAlg} (see
    also~\cite{GrothMetrProjFlatBanSp} and~\cite[proposition 3.2]{HelMetrFrQMod})
    $\overline{P}$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\ellone{\Gamma}$ for some
    set $\Gamma$.

    For the convenience of the reader we sketch the classical argument behind
    the last step. Taking adjoints of $\overline{\pi}$ and $\overline{\sigma}$
    we see that ${\overline{P}}^*$ is a $(1+\varepsilon)$-retract of the
    $1$-injective Banach space $\ell_\infty(B_P)$, so ${\overline{P}}^*$ is
    $(1+\varepsilon)$-injective for every $\varepsilon>0$, that is, every
    bounded linear operator from a subspace of a Banach space into
    ${\overline{P}}^*$ extends to the whole space with norm increased by at
    most $1+\varepsilon$. Passing to the infimum over $\varepsilon$ we get that
    ${\overline{P}}^*$ is $1$-injective, hence isometrically isomorphic to
    $C(K)$ for a Stonean space $K$ \cite[theorem 3.11.6]{LaceyIsomThOfClassicBanSp},
    and by Grothendieck's theorem~\cite{GrothMetrProjFlatBanSp}
    (see~\cite[section 17]{LaceyIsomThOfClassicBanSp}) a Banach space whose
    dual is $1$-injective is isometrically isomorphic to $L_1(\Omega,\mu)$ for
    some measure space $(\Omega,\mu)$. Finally, $\overline{P}$ is isomorphic
    to a complemented subspace of $\ellone{B_P}$ and therefore has the Schur
    property, which forces $\mu$ to be purely atomic, and $L_1$ of a purely
    atomic measure is isometrically isomorphic to $\ellone{\Gamma}$, where
    $\Gamma$ is the set of atoms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{FiniteDimCase} A finite dimensional normed space is
    extremally projective if and only if it is isometrically isomorphic to
    $\ell_1^n$ for some $n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof} A finite dimensional normed space coincides with its completion,
    so the necessity follows from theorem~\ref{CompletionIsEllOne}. The
    sufficiency follows from proposition~\ref{FreeIsExtrProj}, since
    $\ell_1^n=\lo{\{1,\ldots,n\}}$.
\end{proof}

Thus, the examples of section~\ref{SectionCounterexample} are necessarily
infinite dimensional. Theorems~\ref{NoCompleteSubspaces}
and~\ref{CompletionIsEllOne} describe extremally projective normed spaces
from two opposite sides: they are dense subspaces of the spaces
$\ellone{\Gamma}$ which are small enough not to contain infinite dimensional
complete subspaces. In this section we show that for spaces of countable
dimension there is nothing in between: every dense subspace of $\ell_1$ of
countable algebraic dimension is extremally projective, and even almost
isometric to $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$. The proof is a back-and-forth construction
based on theorem~\ref{OneVectorTheorem} and on the following two lemmas.

\begin{lemma}\label{HittingLemma} Let $Y$ be a finite dimensional subspace
    of $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$ be such that $e_n\notin Y$.
    Then there is a constant $C\geq 1$ such that for every
    $z\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ with $\norm{z-e_n}\leq{(4C)}^{-1}$ there exists a
    linear bijection $S:\ell_1\to\ell_1$ with
    $\norm{S-1_{\ell_1}}\leq 2C\norm{z-e_n}\leq 1/2$,
    $S(y)=y$ for all $y\in Y$ and $S(z)=e_n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Since $Y$ is finite dimensional, it is closed in $\ell_1$, so
    $d=\inf\{\norm{e_n-y}:y\in Y\}>0$. By the Hahn-Banach theorem there is
    $f\in\ell_1^*$ with $f(y)=0$ for $y\in Y$, $f(e_n)=1$ and $\norm{f}=d^{-1}$.
    Put $C=\max(1,d^{-1})$. Let $z\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ satisfy
    $\norm{z-e_n}\leq{(4C)}^{-1}$. Then
    $|f(z)-1|=|f(z-e_n)|\leq C\norm{z-e_n}\leq 1/4$, so $|f(z)|\geq 3/4$.
    Define
    \[
    S:\ell_1\to\ell_1,\quad S(x)=x+\frac{f(x)}{f(z)}(e_n-z).
    \]
    Then $S(y)=y$ for $y\in Y$ because $f$ vanishes on $Y$, and
    $S(z)=z+(e_n-z)=e_n$. Moreover,
    $\norm{S-1_{\ell_1}}\leq\norm{f}|f(z)|^{-1}\norm{e_n-z}\leq
    \frac43 C\norm{z-e_n}\leq 2C\norm{z-e_n}\leq 1/2$, so $S$ is a linear
    bijection of $\ell_1$ by the Neumann series.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{ProductLemma} Let $X$ be a Banach space and
    ${(F_m)}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of bounded linear operators on $X$
    such that $\theta_m=\norm{F_m-1_X}<1$ for all $m$ and
    $\sum_{m=1}^\infty\theta_m<+\infty$. Put $A_j=F_jF_{j-1}\cdots F_1$ for
    $j\in\mathbb{N}$. Then the sequence ${(A_j)}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges in
    the operator norm to a linear bijection $T$ of $X$ with
    \[
    \norm{T}\leq\prod_{m=1}^\infty(1+\theta_m),\qquad
    \norm{T^{-1}}\leq\prod_{m=1}^\infty{(1-\theta_m)}^{-1}.
    \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Both products converge because $\sum_m\theta_m<+\infty$; denote
    them by $M$ and $M'$. Then $\norm{A_j}\leq M$ and, since each $F_m$ is
    invertible with $\norm{F_m^{-1}}\leq{(1-\theta_m)}^{-1}$ by the Neumann
    series, $\norm{A_j^{-1}}\leq M'$ for all $j$. From
    $\norm{A_j-A_{j-1}}=\norm{(F_j-1_X)A_{j-1}}\leq\theta_j M$ we see that
    ${(A_j)}_j$ is a Cauchy sequence; let $T$ be its limit, so $\norm{T}\leq M$.
    Also $\norm{A_j^{-1}-A_k^{-1}}=\norm{A_j^{-1}(A_k-A_j)A_k^{-1}}\leq
    {M'}^2\norm{A_k-A_j}$, so ${(A_j^{-1})}_j$ is a Cauchy sequence with a
    limit $B$, $\norm{B}\leq M'$. Passing to the limit in
    $A_jA_j^{-1}=A_j^{-1}A_j=1_X$ we get $TB=BT=1_X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{BackAndForth} Let $D$ be a dense subspace of $\ell_1$ of
    countable algebraic dimension and $\varepsilon>0$. Then there exists a
    linear bijection $T:\ell_1\to\ell_1$ with
    $\norm{T}\norm{T^{-1}}\leq 1+\varepsilon$ and $T(D)=\lo{\mathbb{N}}$. In
    particular, $\BM(D,\lo{\mathbb{N}})=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Since $\ell_1$ is infinite dimensional and $D$ is dense in
    $\ell_1$, the algebraic dimension of $D$ is countably infinite. Let
    ${(d_j)}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a Hamel basis of $D$. Choose a sequence
    ${(\theta_m)}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}$ of numbers in $(0,1/2]$ with
    $\prod_{m=1}^\infty(1+\theta_m){(1-\theta_m)}^{-1}\leq 1+\varepsilon$.

    We construct by induction linear bijections $F_1,F_2,\ldots$ of $\ell_1$
    with $\norm{F_m-1_{\ell_1}}\leq\theta_m$ and finite dimensional subspaces
    $Y_0=\{0\}\subset Y_1\subset Y_2\subset\ldots$ of $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ such
    that, with $A_0=1_{\ell_1}$ and $A_j=F_{2j}F_{2j-1}A_{j-1}$, for every
    $j\in\mathbb{N}$ the following conditions hold:
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\alph*)]
        \item $A_j(d_i)\in Y_j$ for $i\leq j$;
        \item $e_i\in Y_j$ for $i\leq j$;
        \item $Y_j\subset A_j(D)$;
        \item $F_{2j-1}$ and $F_{2j}$ act as the identity on $Y_{j-1}$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Suppose that $A_{j-1}$ and $Y_{j-1}$ have been constructed (for $j=1$
    there is nothing to suppose). Let $N$ be an integer not smaller than every
    element of the supports of the vectors of some basis of $Y_{j-1}$, so that
    $y=y|_N$ for all $y\in Y_{j-1}$. Apply theorem~\ref{OneVectorTheorem} to
    the vector $v=A_{j-1}(d_j)$, the integer $N$ and $\delta=\theta_{2j-1}$,
    and let $F_{2j-1}=1_{\ell_1}-R$ be the resulting operator. Then
    $\norm{F_{2j-1}-1_{\ell_1}}\leq\theta_{2j-1}$, $F_{2j-1}$ acts as the
    identity on $Y_{j-1}$, and $y_j=F_{2j-1}A_{j-1}(d_j)\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$.
    Put $A'=F_{2j-1}A_{j-1}$ and $Y'=Y_{j-1}+\mathbb{C}y_j\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}$.
    Note that $Y'\subset A'(D)$: indeed, $Y_{j-1}\subset A_{j-1}(D)$ by the
    induction hypothesis (for $j=1$ trivially), $F_{2j-1}$ fixes $Y_{j-1}$, so
    $Y_{j-1}=F_{2j-1}(Y_{j-1})\subset A'(D)$, and $y_j=A'(d_j)\in A'(D)$.

    If $e_j\in Y'$, put $F_{2j}=1_{\ell_1}$ and $Y_j=Y'$. Otherwise, let $C$
    be the constant given by lemma~\ref{HittingLemma} for $Y'$ and $n=j$, and
    let $N'$ be an integer not smaller than $j$ and than every element of the
    supports of the vectors of some basis of $Y'$. Since $A'$ is a linear
    bijection of $\ell_1$ and $D$ is dense, $A'(D)$ is dense in $\ell_1$, so
    there is $d\in D$ with
    \[
    \norm{A'(d)-e_j}\leq\frac{\theta_{2j}}{16C}.
    \]
    Apply theorem~\ref{OneVectorTheorem} to the vector $A'(d)$, the integer
    $N'$ and $\delta=\theta_{2j}/2$, and let $G=1_{\ell_1}-R'$ be the
    resulting operator. Then $G$ fixes $Y'$ and $e_j$,
    $\norm{G-1_{\ell_1}}\leq\theta_{2j}/2$, $\norm{G}\leq 5/4$ because
    $\theta_{2j}\leq 1/2$, and $z=GA'(d)\in\lo{\mathbb{N}}$ satisfies
    \[
    \norm{z-e_j}=\norm{G(A'(d)-e_j)}\leq\frac{5\theta_{2j}}{64C}\leq\frac{1}{4C}.
    \]
    Let $S$ be the operator given by lemma~\ref{HittingLemma} for $Y'$, $n=j$
    and $z$. Then $S$ fixes $Y'$, $S(z)=e_j$ and
    $\norm{S-1_{\ell_1}}\leq 2C\norm{z-e_j}\leq 5\theta_{2j}/32$. Put $F_{2j}=SG$
    and $Y_j=Y'+\mathbb{C}e_j$. Then
    \[
    \norm{F_{2j}-1_{\ell_1}}\leq\norm{(S-1_{\ell_1})G}+\norm{G-1_{\ell_1}}
    \leq\frac{5\theta_{2j}}{32}\cdot\frac54+\frac{\theta_{2j}}{2}
    \leq\theta_{2j}.
    \]
    Let us check $(a)$--$(d)$ for $j$. The operator $F_{2j}$ acts as the
    identity on $Y'\supset Y_{j-1}$, which together with the first step gives
    $(d)$. For $i<j$ we have $A_{j-1}(d_i)\in Y_{j-1}$, so
    $A_j(d_i)=F_{2j}F_{2j-1}A_{j-1}(d_i)=A_{j-1}(d_i)\in Y_j$, and
    $A_j(d_j)=F_{2j}(y_j)=y_j\in Y_j$; this is $(a)$. Condition $(b)$ holds
    by the definition of $Y_j$ and the induction hypothesis. Finally,
    $Y'\subset A'(D)$ and $F_{2j}$ fixes $Y'$, so $Y'\subset F_{2j}A'(D)=A_j(D)$,
    and $e_j=SGA'(d)=A_j(d)\in A_j(D)$; this is $(c)$.

    By lemma~\ref{ProductLemma} the operators $A_j$ converge in norm to a
    linear bijection $T$ of $\ell_1$ with
    $\norm{T}\norm{T^{-1}}\leq\prod_m(1+\theta_m){(1-\theta_m)}^{-1}\leq
    1+\varepsilon$. For every $i\in\mathbb{N}$ and $j\geq i$ we have
    $A_j(d_i)\in Y_j$ by $(a)$, and by $(d)$ the operators $F_{2j+1},F_{2j+2},\ldots$
    fix $Y_j$, so $A_k(d_i)=A_j(d_i)$ for all $k\geq j$ and
    $T(d_i)=A_i(d_i)\in Y_i\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}$. Hence
    $T(D)\subset\lo{\mathbb{N}}$. Similarly, for every $i\in\mathbb{N}$ by
    $(b)$ and $(c)$ there is $d\in D$ with $A_i(d)=e_i\in Y_i$, and by $(d)$
    we get $T(d)=A_i(d)=e_i$. Hence $\lo{\mathbb{N}}\subset T(D)$.
\end{proof}

Now we are ready to prove the main result of the paper.

\begin{theorem}\label{MainTheorem} Let $P$ be a normed space of countable
    (finite or countably infinite) algebraic dimension. Then the following
    conditions are equivalent:
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]
        \item $P$ is extremally projective;
        \item $\overline{P}$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\ellone{\Gamma}$
              for some countable set $\Gamma$;
        \item $\BM(P,\lo{\Gamma})=1$ for some countable set $\Gamma$;
        \item for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a set $\Lambda$ with
              $\BM(P,\lo{\Lambda})<1+\varepsilon$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} $(i)\implies(ii)$ By theorem~\ref{CompletionIsEllOne} the space
    $\overline{P}$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\ellone{\Gamma}$ for some
    set $\Gamma$. The rational linear span of a countable Hamel basis of $P$
    is a countable dense subset of $\overline{P}$, so $\overline{P}$ is
    separable and $\Gamma$ is countable.

    $(ii)\implies(iii)$ Let $J:\overline{P}\to\ellone{\Gamma}$ be an isometric
    isomorphism. If $\Gamma$ is finite, then $\overline{P}$ is finite
    dimensional, so $P=\overline{P}$ and $J$ maps $P$ onto
    $\ellone{\Gamma}=\lo{\Gamma}$. If $\Gamma$ is infinite, we may assume that
    $\Gamma=\mathbb{N}$. Then $D=J(P)$ is a dense subspace of $\ell_1$ of
    countable algebraic dimension, and by theorem~\ref{BackAndForth}
    $\BM(P,\lo{\mathbb{N}})=\BM(D,\lo{\mathbb{N}})=1$.

    $(iii)\implies(iv)$ is obvious.

    $(iv)\implies(i)$ Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and a linear bijection
    $S:P\to\lo{\Lambda}$ with $\norm{S}\norm{S^{-1}}<1+\varepsilon$. Then $P$
    is a $(1+\varepsilon)$-retract of $\lo{\Lambda}$ via $S^{-1}$ and $S$, so
    $P$ is extremally projective by theorem~\ref{ExtrProjCriterion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{DenseCountableSubspaces} Every subspace of $\ell_1$
    of countable algebraic dimension which is dense in $\ell_1$ is extremally
    projective and almost isometric to $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$, that is, has
    Banach-Mazur distance $1$ to $\lo{\mathbb{N}}$.
\end{corollary}

Condition $(ii)$ of theorem~\ref{MainTheorem} shows that for normed spaces of
countable dimension extremal projectivity is a property of the completion. By
example~\ref{WeightedExample} this is no longer true for spaces of uncountable
dimension. The following necessary condition explains what goes wrong in the
uncountable case. Recall that a topological space is called a Baire space if
the intersection of every sequence of its open dense subsets is dense; every
non-empty open subset of a Baire space is a Baire space, and complete metric
spaces and compact Hausdorff spaces are Baire spaces.

\begin{definition}\label{BaireLFD} We say that a normed space $P$ is
    Baire-locally finite dimensional if for every Baire space $K$, every
    continuous map $\gamma:K\to P$ and every non-empty open set $W\subset K$
    there is a non-empty open set $U\subset W$ such that $\gamma(U)$ is
    contained in a finite dimensional subspace of $P$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{BaireLFDProps}
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]
        \item Every normed space of countable algebraic dimension is
              Baire-locally finite dimensional.
        \item If $P$ is Baire-locally finite dimensional and $Q$ is a normed
              space admitting an injective bounded linear operator
              $\sigma:Q\to P$, then $Q$ is Baire-locally finite dimensional.
        \item For every set $\Lambda$ the space $\lo{\Lambda}$ is
              Baire-locally finite dimensional.
        \item Every topologically projective normed space is Baire-locally
              finite dimensional.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} $(i)$ Let $P=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_n$ be an increasing union
    of finite dimensional subspaces, $\gamma:K\to P$ be continuous and
    $W\subset K$ be non-empty and open. The sets $\gamma^{-1}(E_n)\cap W$ are
    closed in $W$ and cover $W$, so by the Baire category theorem one of them
    has non-empty interior $U$ in $W$, and $\gamma(U)\subset E_n$.

    $(ii)$ If $\gamma:K\to Q$ is continuous, then so is $\sigma\gamma:K\to P$.
    If $\sigma\gamma(U)$ is contained in a finite dimensional subspace $E$ of
    $P$, then $\gamma(U)\subset\sigma^{-1}(E)$, which is finite dimensional
    because $\sigma$ is injective.

    $(iii)$ Let $\gamma:K\to\lo{\Lambda}$ be continuous and $W\subset K$ be
    non-empty and open. For $k\in K$ put $s(k)=|\supp(\gamma(k))|$. For every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$ the function $k\mapsto\gamma(k)(\lambda)$ is
    continuous, so the set $\{k\in K:\gamma(k)(\lambda)\neq 0\}$ is open.
    Therefore for every $m$ the set
    \[
    \{k\in K:s(k)\geq m\}=\bigcup_{F\subset\Lambda,\,|F|=m}\ \bigcap_{\lambda\in F}
    \{k\in K:\gamma(k)(\lambda)\neq 0\}
    \]
    is open. In particular, the sets $K_m=\{k\in W:s(k)\leq m\}$,
    $m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$, are closed in $W$, and they cover $W$ because
    the elements of $\lo{\Lambda}$ are finitely supported. Since $W$ is a
    Baire space, some $K_m$ contains a non-empty open set $V$. Let
    $m'=\max\{s(k):k\in V\}\leq m$ and $U=\{k\in V:s(k)\geq m'\}$. Then $U$ is
    open, non-empty, and $s(k)=m'$ for all $k\in U$. Fix $k_0\in U$ and put
    $F=\supp(\gamma(k_0))$, so $|F|=m'$. The set
    $U_0=\{k\in U:\gamma(k)(\lambda)\neq 0\text{ for all }\lambda\in F\}$ is
    an open neighbourhood of $k_0$. For $k\in U_0$ we have
    $F\subset\supp(\gamma(k))$ and $|\supp(\gamma(k))|=m'=|F|$, so
    $\supp(\gamma(k))=F$ and $\gamma(k)\in\Lin\{e_\lambda:\lambda\in F\}$.
    Thus, $U_0\subset W$ is a non-empty open set with $\gamma(U_0)$ contained
    in a finite dimensional subspace.

    $(iv)$ If $P$ is topologically projective, then by
    proposition~\ref{CanonicalMorphismCriterion} there is a bounded linear
    operator $\sigma:P\to\lo{B_P}$ with $\pi_P\sigma=1_P$, which is injective.
    It remains to apply $(ii)$ and $(iii)$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{NoCompleteSubspaces} is a special case of
proposition~\ref{BaireLFDProps}~$(iv)$: if $Z\subset P$ is infinite
dimensional and complete with respect to a norm dominating the norm of $P$,
then the inclusion $(Z,\norm{\cdot}')\to P$ is a continuous map from a Baire
space, and no non-empty open subset of an infinite dimensional normed space
is contained in a finite dimensional subspace. Here is an example of a
different nature.

\begin{example}\label{CurveExample} For $0<\alpha<1$ let
    $v_\alpha={(\alpha^n)}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_1$, and let
    \[
    P=\lo{\mathbb{N}}+\Lin\{v_\alpha:1/3\leq\alpha\leq 2/3\}\subset\ell_1
    \]
    with the norm inherited from $\ell_1$. Then $P$ is a dense subspace of
    $\ell_1$ which is not topologically projective.
\end{example}
\begin{proof} The map $\gamma:[1/3,2/3]\to P$, $\alpha\mapsto v_\alpha$, is
    continuous, because
    $\norm{v_\alpha-v_\beta}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty|\alpha^n-\beta^n|
    \leq|\alpha-\beta|\sum_{n=1}^\infty n{(2/3)}^{n-1}$. Let $U$ be a non-empty
    open subset of the compact space $[1/3,2/3]$. Then $U$ is infinite, and
    for pairwise distinct $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m\in U$ the vectors
    $v_{\alpha_1},\ldots,v_{\alpha_m}$ are linearly independent: if
    $\sum_{i=1}^m c_iv_{\alpha_i}=0$, then $\sum_{i=1}^m c_i\alpha_i^n=0$ for
    $n=1,\ldots,m$, and the Vandermonde matrix ${(\alpha_i^n)}_{i,n=1}^m$ is
    invertible, so all $c_i$ vanish. Hence $\gamma(U)$ is not contained in a
    finite dimensional subspace, $P$ is not Baire-locally finite dimensional,
    and by proposition~\ref{BaireLFDProps}~$(iv)$ it is not topologically
    projective.
\end{proof}

Note that example~\ref{CurveExample} is of the form $\lo{\mathbb{N}}+V$, as
the extremally projective spaces of theorem~\ref{FiniteDimPerturbation} and
corollary~\ref{DenseCountableSubspaces}, but now $V$ has uncountable
dimension. On the other hand, uncountable dimension alone is not an
obstruction.

\begin{proposition}\label{UncountableExamples} Let $\Gamma$ be a set and
    $V$ be a subspace of $\ellone{\Gamma}$ of countable algebraic dimension.
    Then $\lo{\Gamma}+V$ is extremally projective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} The set $\Gamma_0=\bigcup\{\supp(v):v\in V\}$ is countable,
    being the union of the supports of the vectors of a countable Hamel basis
    of $V$. Put $P_0=\lo{\Gamma_0}+V\subset\ellone{\Gamma_0}$. Then
    $\lo{\Gamma}+V=\lo{\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_0}\bigoplus_1 P_0$ isometrically.
    The space $P_0$ has countable dimension and its completion is
    $\ellone{\Gamma_0}$, so it is extremally projective by
    theorem~\ref{MainTheorem}. By propositions~\ref{FreeIsExtrProj}
    and~\ref{SumsOfExtrProj} the space $\lo{\Gamma}+V$ is extremally projective.
\end{proof}

By theorem~\ref{CompletionIsEllOne} and proposition~\ref{BaireLFDProps}, an
extremally projective normed space is a Baire-locally finite dimensional
dense subspace of some $\ellone{\Gamma}$. For spaces of countable dimension
the converse holds by theorem~\ref{MainTheorem}, since such spaces are
automatically Baire-locally finite dimensional. We do not know whether the
converse holds in general.

\begin{question}\label{OpenQuestion} Let $\Gamma$ be an uncountable set and
    $P$ be a dense subspace of $\ellone{\Gamma}$ which is Baire-locally finite
    dimensional. Is $P$ extremally projective? Is $\BM(P,\lo{\Gamma})=1$?
\end{question}
